All-over print looks like a decoration choice. It is actually a fabric choice. The process only works on certain fibres, and the fabric you pick changes the colour, the sharpness and the hand feel of the finished bag more than any setting on the printer does.

This guide covers what we print on, what each fabric does to your design, and — because it is the honest answer sometimes — when all-over print is the wrong method for what you are trying to achieve.

> Already decided on all-over print and ready to send files? See our **Artwork Preparation Guide** for formats, resolution, colour and bleed.

## How the Process Works

All-over print on our line is **dye sublimation**. Your design is printed onto transfer paper, then heat and pressure turn the ink into a gas that bonds into the polyester fibre. The colour becomes part of the fabric rather than sitting on top of it.

**What that gives you:**

- Unlimited colours at no extra cost per colour — a photographic design costs the same per piece as a two-colour one
- Edge-to-edge coverage, including over seams and into corners
- No cracking, no peeling, no fading of the print itself
- No change to the hand feel — the fabric feels exactly as it did before printing

**The three hard limits:**

| Limit | What it means |
|---|---|
| Polyester only | Sublimation bonds to polyester and high-polyester blends. It does not work on cotton or canvas |
| No white ink | White in your design means unprinted fabric. Every colour is ink laid onto a white base |
| Cannot lighten fabric | A pale design cannot be printed onto a dark base. Dark areas are printed as part of the artwork, with light elements left as bare fabric |

**Printed first, cut second.** Fabric is printed flat, then cut and sewn into a bag. This is why placement planning matters and why a die-line matters — covered in the Artwork Preparation Guide.

## Fabrics We Print On

| Fabric | Character | Print result | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300D polyester | Light, smooth, economical | Bright and sharp | Promotional bags, drawstring bags, light totes |
| 600D polyester | The workhorse — structured, durable | Bright, very slightly softened by texture | Backpacks, waist bags, everyday retail |
| 900D–1200D polyester | Heavy, stiff, hard-wearing | Good colour, texture visible in fine detail | Heavy-duty and outdoor styles |
| Polyester canvas | Cotton-like appearance, matte | Softer, more muted — a deliberate look | Tote bags, lifestyle and heritage styling |
| Ripstop polyester | Light with a visible grid weave | Grid is visible through the print | Outdoor and technical styles |
| Neoprene-feel poly | Soft, padded, stretchy | Very saturated colour | Cosmetic bags, sleeves, soft pouches |
| Poly mesh | Open weave | Partial coverage by nature | Panels and accents, not main bodies |

**Texture softens detail.** The smoother the fabric, the sharper the print. A heavy textured weave will slightly break up hairline detail and small text — not a defect, just physics. If your design depends on fine linework, specify a smoother base.

**Coated fabrics need checking first.** Some PU and PVC coatings interfere with sublimation. Tell us if your specification includes a coating and we will confirm compatibility before you commit.

## What Fabric Does to Your Colour

The same file prints differently on different bases, and this surprises buyers more than anything else in the process.

- **Smooth and light fabrics give the most saturated colour.** This is why 300D and neoprene-feel polyester look the most vivid.
- **Textured fabrics mute colour slightly** because the surface scatters light. Polyester canvas is the clearest example — designs read softer and more matte, which is often exactly what a lifestyle brand wants.
- **Blends dilute colour.** Sublimation only bonds to the polyester content. An 80/20 blend prints noticeably softer than 100% polyester, because a fifth of the fibre takes no dye at all. If you want full saturation, specify 100% polyester.
- **Fabric weight does not change colour much.** 300D and 600D of the same weave print very similarly. Weave and blend matter far more than denier here.

**Approve colour on a physical sample, never on screen.** This is the single most useful sentence in this guide. Send Pantone coated codes as your target and check the result in your hands.

## When All-Over Print Is Not the Right Answer

We would rather tell you this before you order.

| Your situation | Better method |
|---|---|
| The bag must be cotton or canvas | Screen printing or heat transfer on the actual fabric |
| One placed logo on a plain body | Screen printing, embroidery or a patch — cheaper and faster |
| A light logo on a dark bag | Embroidery, patch, or a dyed body with applied branding |
| You need an exact Pantone match | Pantone fabric dyeing for the body, with applied branding |
| Very small quantity, simple mark | Heat transfer or embroidery — no full-panel setup |
| The design must survive extreme abrasion at one point | The print itself is permanent, but consider a patch or badge for a focal mark |

**Where all-over print genuinely wins:** full-coverage patterns, photographic or gradient artwork, many-colour designs, camouflage and repeat patterns, team and event kits where every piece is different, and any design that needs to run past a seam without interruption.

## FAQ

**What fabrics can be all-over printed?**
Dye sublimation bonds to polyester and high-polyester blends only. We print on 300D to 1200D polyester, polyester canvas, ripstop polyester, neoprene-feel polyester and poly mesh. It does not work on cotton or canvas made from natural fibres.

**Why can't you all-over print on cotton?**
Sublimation ink bonds chemically to polyester fibre. Cotton has nothing for it to bond to, so the colour washes out. If your project requires cotton, screen printing or heat transfer on the actual fabric is the right method instead.

**Can you print a light design onto a dark bag?**
No. There is no white ink in sublimation and the ink cannot lighten fabric, so light elements have to be unprinted fabric. The usual solution is to print the dark area as part of your artwork and leave the light elements bare. Send us your design and we will confirm what is achievable.

**Does all-over print cost more for more colours?**
No. Sublimation prints full colour in a single pass, so a photographic design costs no more per piece than a two-colour one. This is the main advantage over screen printing, where each colour adds a screen and a pass.

**Will the print crack, peel or fade?**
The colour bonds into the fibre rather than sitting on the surface, so there is nothing to crack or peel and the print does not wear off. The fabric itself will age with use, as any fabric does.

**Does the fabric change how my colours look?**
Yes, noticeably. Smooth, light fabrics give the most saturated colour. Textured fabrics such as polyester canvas mute it slightly and read more matte. Blends print softer than 100% polyester because only the polyester content takes dye. Confirm colour on a physical sample rather than on screen.

**Can I get a printed swatch before ordering?**
Yes, and we recommend it, especially if you are choosing between a smooth polyester and a textured one. Contact Anna with your fabric options and your artwork.

**Does the print change how the bag feels?**
No. Because the dye bonds into the fibre rather than forming a layer on top, the fabric feels the same after printing as before. This is the main difference in hand feel between sublimation and a heavy screen print or transfer film.
